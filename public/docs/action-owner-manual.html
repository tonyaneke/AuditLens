Who this manual is for

You have an Action Owner account. Internal Audit raises observations against your department and names an action owner for each one; everyone in the department sees them, and any of you can respond here — describing what was done, attaching the evidence, and sending the item back for verification.

Internal Audit staff and the Head of Internal Audit use the full AuditLens User Manual, which also covers raising observations, the assessment modules and administration. Ask Internal Audit for it if you need it.

If you get stuck. Every observation names the auditor who raised it. Contact them, or the Head of Internal Audit, rather than working around the system — a note left on the observation itself is always visible to both of you.

Section 1

Getting Started

AuditLens is where Internal Audit records what it found and where you record what your department did about it. As an Action Owner you have one job in the system: respond to the observations raised against your department, attach the evidence, and send each one back for verification.

1.1What you are expected to do

An observation is raised against a department, not against one person. It is assigned to a named action owner — the person Internal Audit holds accountable for it — but everyone in that department sees it, and any of you can answer it and send it for closure. That is deliberate: an observation should not sit untouched because one person is on leave or has moved on.

  1. Read the observation — the finding, the criteria it was measured against, the impact, and the recommendation.
  2. Carry out the remediation in your department.
  3. Record what you did in AuditLens and attach the evidence.
  4. Mark it Ready for Closure. Internal Audit verifies it and the Head of Internal Audit closes it.
Shared visibility, single accountability. Everyone in the department can read, comment on and close out any of its observations, and every step is recorded against the person who took it — so the trail always shows who did what. The named owner remains the person Internal Audit will ask.
You do not close observations yourself. Marking an item Ready for Closure sends it for verification; it is closed by the Head of Internal Audit after an auditor has checked the evidence. See 3.1.

1.2How to sign in

  1. In your browser, open the AuditLens address supplied by Internal Audit.
  2. Click Sign in with Microsoft.
  3. Complete Microsoft sign-in with your @credicorp.ng work account.
  4. If sign-in succeeds you are taken to your dashboard.
    Your name, role and profile photo are read from your Microsoft account and shown at the bottom of the side navigation bar. There is no separate AuditLens password to remember.
If sign-in is refused. "Your Microsoft account isn't set up in AuditLens yet" means no account has been created for you — ask the Head of Internal Audit to add you. "Your AuditLens account is inactive" means your access has been suspended; contact the Head of Internal Audit.
Figure 1: The AuditLens sign-in page. Sign in with your work account — there is no separate AuditLens password.

1.3How to sign out

  1. Go to the bottom of the side navigation bar, where your name and role are shown.
  2. Click Sign out.

1.4Your dashboard

The dashboard is the first view after sign-in, and is reached at any time by clicking Dashboard at the top of the side navigation bar.

You see a single Needs attention list, drawing together every internal observation raised against your department, plus the external findings and fraud prevention actions assigned to you personally — with its type, the audit it came from, its priority, its expected close date and its current status. Click any row to open the item and respond. If the list is empty, nothing is outstanding.

The counters above it read the same way: My department is everything your department is carrying, and Open is the part of it still awaiting a response from someone in the department — not necessarily you.

1.5The side navigation bar

The side navigation bar is on the left of every screen. Your items are in the Portal group:

ItemWhat it holds
Internal ObservationsFindings raised against your department by Internal Audit — all of them, whoever they are assigned to.
External ObservationsFindings raised by regulators and external auditors, assigned to you personally.
Fraud Risk Control TrackerFraud prevention actions assigned to you, each with its control type, target date and status.

A red count on a Portal item shows how much is still open and awaiting a response. On Internal Observations that count is your whole department's, because any of you can answer any of them; on the other two it is yours alone. Items already sent back to Internal Audit are not counted — so the number falling to zero means nothing is with the department, not that everything is closed.

The rest of AuditLens — the audit files, the reports, the assessment modules and administration — belongs to Internal Audit and is not shown to you. There is nothing missing from your side navigation bar.

1.6Notifications

The bell icon in the top bar carries a count of unread notifications — an observation raised against your department, a reply on one of its items, or a request for a progress report. Click the bell to open the list, then click any entry to go straight to the item.

Everyone in the department gets the bell when an observation is raised against it and again when it is closed. Email goes to the named action owner for that observation, so one item does not put the same message in six inboxes; if you are not the named owner, the bell is where you will see it.

Section 2

Your Observations

Your department's work sits in the Portal group of the side navigation bar. All three lists behave the same way: open an item, read it, respond to it, attach evidence, and send it back.

2.1Finding your observations

  1. Click Internal Observations in the Portal group.
  2. The observations raised against your department are split into those needing a response and those already with Internal Audit. Start with the first list — the second is waiting on someone else.
  3. Click a row to open the observation.

Each row shows the criticality, the audit the finding came from, the expected close date and the current status, so you can work through the list worst-first or by date without opening every item. It also names the action owner, or says Assigned to you where the item is yours.

The list holds every observation raised against your department, not only the ones in your name. Use the Show filter at the top to switch between:

ShowWhat you get
My departmentEverything raised against your department. This is the default.
Assigned to meOnly the observations naming you as action owner or co-owner.

The Status and Criticality filters narrow either view further.

Figure 2: The Internal Observations portal. The Portal group in the side navigation bar carries a count of the items still awaiting your response.

2.2Reading an observation

An observation is written in four parts. Read all four before responding — the recommendation is what your response will be judged against.

PartWhat it tells you
ObservationWhat was found.
CriteriaThe policy, procedure or standard it was measured against.
ImpactWhat the finding exposes the Corporation to.
RecommendationWhat Internal Audit expects to be done. Your response should address this line for line.

The observation also names the auditor who raised it, its criticality rating (see 5.1), and its expected close date.

2.3Asking a question or reporting progress

Use the comment box on the observation to ask the auditor a question, report interim progress, or attach a document — without closing anything. The auditor who raised the item, the lead auditor and the Head of Internal Audit all see it.

Note. Comments marked private are visible to your department only — they are not shared with Internal Audit. Everything else in the conversation is. Anyone in the department can comment on any of its observations and anyone can mark one Ready for Closure, whether or not they are the named owner.

2.4External Observations

  1. Click External Observations in the Portal group.
  2. Review the findings raised against your department by regulators and external auditors.
  3. Open a finding, respond, attach evidence and mark it ready for closure — the workflow is the same one described in Section 3.

2.5Fraud Risk Control Tracker

  1. Click Fraud Risk Control Tracker in the Portal group.
  2. Review the fraud prevention actions assigned to you, each with its control type, target date and status.
  3. Open an action to record progress, then click Mark completed when it is done.

Section 3

Responding & Closing

This is the part of AuditLens you will use most. Every observation is closed through the same four stages, and only the second one is your department's. The Remediation & verification block on the observation always shows where the item stands and what happens next.

3.1The closure workflow

Stage 1
Raised
Internal Audit raises the observation against your department and names an action owner.
Stage 2
Ready for closure
Your department's. Anyone in it remediates, responds and attaches evidence.
Stage 3
Auditor verified
Internal Audit reviews your response and verifies it for closure.
Stage 4
Head verified & closed
The Head of Internal Audit signs off and the observation is closed.

The stepper on each observation marks completed stages with ✓ and names who completed them and when. A stage that has been sent back is marked ↩ returned rather than reverting to blank, so a rejection is never mistaken for a fresh start — and the note giving the reason sits directly beneath the stepper.

Beneath the stepper, a Next step line always states what has to happen next and who has to do it. If you are unsure where an item stands, read that line first.

Figure 3: The Remediation & verification block. Here stage 2 shows ↩ Returned — the item was sent back to the action owner, and the note giving the reason sits directly beneath the stepper.

3.2Mark an observation Ready for Closure

  1. Click Internal Observations in the Portal group, or open the item from your dashboard or a notification.
  2. Open the observation and read the description, criteria, impact and recommendation.
  3. Carry out the remediation in your department.
  4. Click ✓ Ready for Closure.
  5. In Your closure response, describe what your department actually did to address the finding.
  6. Click Attach file to add supporting evidence — the revised procedure, the signed memo, the system screenshot, the training register.
  7. Click Submit. Your response is checked before it is sent — see 3.3.
Do this once the work is actually done. Ready for Closure means the remediation is complete and the evidence exists. To report that work is under way, leave a comment instead (2.3) — it keeps the auditor informed without starting a verification that will only be sent back.
Whoever did the work submits it. You do not have to be the named action owner to respond. Your name is recorded on the response and appears on the stepper as the person who marked it Ready for Closure, so the department can share the work without losing the trail. Check the conversation before you start, in case a colleague is already drafting a response.
Figure 4: The Mark Ready for Closure dialog.

3.3The closure response check

When you press Submit, AuditLens reads your response before it reaches the auditor. The point is to save you a rejection: a response that only says the work is done gets sent back by the auditor anyway, and that round trip costs days.

What is checked

Two checks run, in order.

  1. Unfilled gaps. If your response still contains square-bracket placeholders such as [date the matrix took effect], submission stops and the remaining gaps are listed. Replace each one, brackets and all, with your own details. This check always applies — it is not a judgement about quality, it simply means the response is not finished.
  2. Specifics. The response is then read for whether it concretely addresses the recommendation. Any PDF or Word evidence you attached is read as part of your submission, so detail that lives in the document counts even if it is not repeated in the box.

A response is sent back to you for more detail when it:

  • only asserts completion — "done", "noted", "resolved", "we have complied" — without saying what was done;
  • restates the recommendation back as though quoting it were evidence of having acted on it;
  • promises rather than reports — "will be", "is being", "in progress", "shortly";
  • names no specific document, action, date or owner an auditor could go and check.

What you get back

If more detail is needed, the dialog shows four things:

What is shownWhat to do with it
FeedbackA sentence or two naming exactly what is missing.
QuestionsThe specific points your response has not yet answered.
Ready-to-send wordingA complete draft response with the facts left as [bold gaps]. Click Use this template to copy it into the response box word for word, then replace each gap with your own details. Everything outside the brackets is final wording — you should only be typing facts.
Things to noteShort notes on filling the gaps without being sent back again — give a real date rather than "recently", attach the documents the template names, and so on.
Figure 5: The closure response check. The response submitted was "Noted. This has been resolved and we have complied." — the check names what is missing, then supplies finished wording with the facts left as bracketed gaps.

3.4After you submit

When the response passes, the dialog confirms ✓ Looks concrete and submits. The observation moves to stage 2, and the auditor who raised it, the lead auditor and the Head of Internal Audit are all notified in the app and by email. It leaves your needing your response list and the red count in the side navigation bar falls by one.

One of two things then happens:

  • It is verified and closed. The auditor checks your evidence and sends it to the Head of Internal Audit, who signs it off. The observation is stamped with its closure date and moves to Closed. Nothing further is required of you.
  • It comes back to you. The stepper shows ↩ returned with a note explaining what more is needed, and the item reappears in your list. Add what was asked for and mark it Ready for Closure again — the same dialog, and your previous response is still there to build on.
Being asked for a progress report. Internal Audit can request an interim update without closing anything. A banner stays on the observation until you mark it Ready for Closure. Answer it in the comment box (2.3) — a progress report is not a closure response.

3.5If you disagree with an observation

Where you believe an observation is not valid, it can be put forward for withdrawal. Withdrawal requires the Head of Internal Audit's sign-off — you cannot remove an observation yourself.

  1. Open the observation and click Request review.
  2. State the reason the observation is considered invalid, and submit.
  3. Internal Audit reviews the request and either:
    • Forwards it for withdrawal — it goes to the Head of Internal Audit for sign-off; or
    • Declines the request — the observation remains active and the reason is recorded.
  4. Where it is forwarded, the Head of Internal Audit either approves the withdrawal or does not. Both outcomes are recorded on the observation with the reason given.
Note. A withdrawn observation is neither Open nor Closed. It is retained in full with its reason and sign-off, and is listed separately from the active observations on the report. A request that is declined leaves the observation exactly as it was — still with your department, still awaiting a response.

Section 4

Getting It Right

The short answers to the things action owners most often ask, and the handful of habits that keep items from coming back.

4.1Common questions

QuestionAnswer
Why do I see observations that are not assigned to me? Because they were raised against your department, and anyone in it can answer them. Set Show to Assigned to me (2.1) to see only your own.
Can I answer an observation assigned to a colleague? Yes — if it is your department's, you can respond to it and mark it Ready for Closure. Your name is recorded on the response. Check the conversation first in case they have already started.
I cannot see an observation I was told about. It may not have been approved yet — an observation only reaches the department once the Head of Internal Audit approves it. It may also be against a different department: you see your own department's items only. Ask the auditor who raised it.
My department is wrong in AuditLens. Tell the Head of Internal Audit. Your department is what decides which observations you see, so an out-of-date one shows you the wrong list.
I cover two departments. Both can be set on your account, and both show you the same full access — you will see the observations of each. Ask the Head of Internal Audit to add the second one.
Can I edit an observation? No. Internal Audit owns the wording of the finding. If something in it is wrong, say so in the comment box or use Request review (3.5).
Can I change the expected close date? Not directly. Explain the position in the comment box and ask the auditor to revise it.
Someone else in my department did the work. They can respond themselves — everyone in the department can. Otherwise respond on their behalf and name them in the response.
I am going on leave. Your colleagues can already see and answer your department's observations, so nothing stalls. For a long absence, tell Internal Audit — they can reassign the named owner.
What file types can I attach? Documents and images. PDF and Word evidence is read as part of the closure response check (3.3), so detail inside those files counts.
My response was sent back a second time. Read the note on the stepper — it names what is still missing. If it is not clear, ask the auditor in the comment box rather than resubmitting the same wording.
Can I print or save an item? Press Ctrl+P on any page, including this manual, and choose Save as PDF.

4.2Five things that keep items from coming back

  1. Answer the recommendation, not the observation. The recommendation is what closure is measured against.
  2. Give a date. Almost every response sent back is missing one.
  3. Attach the evidence you name. A response that refers to a revised procedure without attaching it will be sent back for it.
  4. Report, do not promise. "Will be implemented" is not a closure response — leave it as a comment until it is done.
  5. Do not wait for the deadline to say something. A comment reporting progress costs nothing and stops the item being chased.

4.3If you get stuck

Every observation names the auditor who raised it. Contact them, or the Head of Internal Audit, rather than working around the system — a note left on the observation itself is always visible to both of you, and it stays with the item as part of its record.

Reference

The ratings, statuses and timelines you will see on your items, and what each of them means for you.

5.1Criticality ratings

Every observation carries one of five ratings. The rating is set by Internal Audit and drives how quickly a response is expected.

RatingDefinition
CriticalImmediate remediation Contravenes the Corporation's zero risk-tolerance indicators and risk appetite statement. Requires immediate remediation.
HighImmediate remediation Significant vulnerability with potentially severe consequences if unaddressed — a major control failure, a regulatory breach or a critical weakness. Requires immediate action and close monitoring by executive management and the Board Audit Committee.
ModerateShort-term, 1–3 months Notable weakness causing inefficiencies and moderate financial or reputational harm if unresolved. Timely corrective action prevents escalation.
LowMedium-term, 4–6 months Minor control weakness or process deviation with minimal operational impact. Manageable through routine improvement.
Process
ImprovementFlexible timing		 An opportunity to improve efficiency and adopt best practice rather than a risk.

This guide is also shown inside AuditLens under How to use AuditLens.

StatusMeaningIs it with your department?

In ProgressRemediation is under way.Yes

WithdrawnApproved for withdrawal by the Head of Internal Audit. Neither Open nor Closed, and listed separately.No

5.3Resolution timelines

TimelineExpected closure
ImmediateWithin 30 days
Short-termWithin 60 days
Long-termWithin 120 days

Where no closure date is set on your observation, the timeline determines the expected close date shown against it. Open observations that are overdue, or due within two weeks, are on the watchlist Internal Audit reviews — which is when you can expect to be chased.
