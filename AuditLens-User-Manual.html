Section 1

Getting Started

AuditLens is CREDICORP's internal audit workspace. It replaces spreadsheet-driven audit working papers with a single browser-based system covering audit planning, observations, remediation tracking, risk assessment and reporting.

1.1About AuditLens

The system carries an audit from planning through to closure. An audit engagement holds one or more reports; each report holds the observations raised during fieldwork. Every observation is assigned to an action owner — a department head accountable for remediating it — and then moves through a controlled closure workflow that ends with the Head of Internal Audit's sign-off.

Alongside this core loop, AuditLens holds the annual risk-based audit plan, the fraud risk register, process reviews, the external findings register, the internal audit self-assessment, and the Executive Assurance Brief issued to the MD and EXCO.

1.2Roles and access

Your role decides which sections appear in your side navigation bar and which actions you can take. There are four roles:

RoleWho holds itWhat they can do
Head of Audit Head, Internal Audit Full access. Raises observations directly, approves everything staff submit, closes observations, and administers users and departments in Settings.
Audit Staff Auditors and interns in Internal Audit Dashboard, Audits & Reports and the Remediation Tracker, plus any assessment sections granted to them individually. Submits observations, edits and deletions for the Head's approval. Cannot open Settings or the Audit log.
Action Owner Department heads with observations against them Sees only their own dashboard and the three Portal sections. Responds to observations, attaches evidence and marks items Ready for Closure.
Admin System administrator Holds Head-of-Audit authority and can additionally switch view to any other role to see the system as that user sees it.

Appendix C sets out the full permission matrix.

Note. An Admin's sidebar shows a role switcher below their name. Switching view changes what is displayed and which actions are offered; it does not reduce the account's underlying authority, and the sidebar continues to show (Admin) after the acting role.

  1. In your browser, open the AuditLens address supplied by Internal Audit.
  2. Click Sign in with Microsoft.
  3. Complete Microsoft sign-in with your @credicorp.ng work account.
  4. If sign-in succeeds you are taken to your dashboard.
    Your name, role and profile photo are read from your Microsoft account and shown at the bottom of the side navigation bar.
If sign-in is refused. "Your Microsoft account isn't set up in AuditLens yet" means no account has been created for you — ask the Head of Internal Audit to add you (see 9.1). "Your AuditLens account is inactive" means your access has been suspended; contact the Head of Internal Audit.

1.5The dashboard

The dashboard is the first view after sign-in, and is reached at any time by clicking Dashboard at the top of the side navigation bar. What it shows depends on your role.

For Internal Audit (Head of Audit and Audit Staff)

  • Welcome banner — your name and role, with the + New Observation button on the right.
  • Headline figures — Audits (and the number of reports beneath them), Observations (open and closed), Key exposures (open Critical and High), and Remediation rate.
  • Observations by criticality — a doughnut chart with counts and percentages for each rating.
  • Risk by process / department — observation counts per audit area, worst-rated area first.
  • Top open Critical & High exposures — the items needing attention now.
  • Expected close timeline — how open observations are distributed across Overdue, ≤ 2 weeks, 2–4 weeks, 1–3 months and > 3 months.
  • Watchlist — open observations due within two weeks. Click a row to expand full detail and add a note.
  • Repeat findings — observations flagged as recurrences of prior findings.
Note. Headline figures count approved observations only. An observation still awaiting the Head of Audit's approval is not included until it is approved, so the dashboard and the Remediation Tracker always agree.

For Action Owners

Action owners see a single Needs your attention list drawing together every internal observation, external finding and fraud prevention action assigned to them, with its type, source audit, priority, expected close date and current status. Click any row to open the item and respond.

Audits & Reports

An audit engagement is the container for everything done on one audit. Beneath it sit the reports issued from that engagement, and beneath each report sit its observations. Set this structure up first — an observation cannot be raised until a report exists to hold it.

2.1Create an audit engagement

Note. Only the Head of Audit can create an audit engagement.
  1. Click Audits & Reports in the side navigation bar.
  2. Click + New audit at the top right. The New Audit Engagement dialog opens.
  3. Complete the fields. Those marked * are compulsory.
    • Audit Engagement Name * — for example, Credit Portfolio Audit.
    • Type — Process or Department.
    • Process / Department — the audit area. This drives the dashboard's risk-by-area breakdown.
    • Period Under Review — for example, October 2025 – December 2025.
    • Lead Auditor — the lead auditor may verify remediation on any observation in this engagement.
    • Engagement Status — Planned, In progress, Completed or On hold.
  4. Click Create Audit.

The audits list can be filtered by Year, Quarter and Status, searched from the top bar, and switched between grid (▦) and table (▤) views.

2.2Build the audit plan

Each engagement carries a plan: scope, objectives, key risks and a test programme. Open the audit from the list to reach it.

Enter the scope, objectives and key risks

  1. Open the audit engagement.
  2. In the Audit Planning — Scope & Test Programme card, click Edit scope, objectives & risks.
  3. Complete Scope, Audit objectives (one per line) and Key risks (one per line).
  4. Save.

Draft the plan with AI

  1. Open the audit engagement.
  2. Click ✦ Generate audit plan.
  3. Add any Additional context that should shape the plan. This is optional.
  4. Click Generate audit plan. A drafted scope, objectives, key risks and test programme are written into the audit.
  5. Review and edit everything produced before relying on it.
Important. AI-generated content throughout AuditLens is a first draft, not a work product. You remain responsible for the accuracy of anything you keep. Review it against your evidence before it forms part of a report.

Add a test to the programme

  1. Open the audit engagement.
  2. In the test programme table, click + Add test.
  3. Complete the fields: Ref, Test name *, Objective, Procedure, Control / assertion tested, Population, Sample basis, Result, Tested by, Test date, What was found, and the Evidence / working-paper reference.
  4. Set Result to Not Tested, Passed, Exception, Partial or N/A.
  5. Save.

A test recorded as Exception or Partial can be turned straight into an observation — see 3.1. The card summarises results as a count of passed, exceptions, partial and not tested.

2.3Terms of Reference

  1. Open the audit engagement.
  2. Click 📄 Terms of Reference at the top right.
  3. Complete Addressed To, Date, Planned Timing, Background & Purpose, Out of Scope Areas, Information & Access Required and Reporting Channel.
  4. Save.

2.4Add a report

  1. Open the audit engagement.
  2. In the Reports card, click + Add Report.
  3. Complete the fields:
    • Report Title * — for example, Q1 Internal Audit Report.
    • Ref No. — the next reference in the IA/<year>/<nnn> series is suggested.
    • Period, Status, Report Type and Scope Description.
  4. Click Create Report.

Open a report to see its findings summary, its observations, and the roll-up of proposed SOP updates. Observations can be filtered by criticality and status, or searched by reference, title, category and description.

2.5Executive summary

Each report carries the narrative that forms Section 1 of the issued audit report.

  1. Open the report.
  2. In the Executive Summary card, click Edit Executive Summary.
  3. Complete the fields:
    • Audit Objective and Areas Out of Scope
    • Highlights of Strengths
    • Areas for Strategic Improvement
    • Overall Audit Assurance Level — High, Moderate, Limited or No assurance
    • Internal Audit Opinion and Conclusion
  4. Click Save Executive Summary.

To draft it from the observations already in the report, click ✦ Generate exec summary in the same card, then review and edit what is produced.

2.6Export to Word

  1. Open the audit engagement (for the whole engagement) or a report (for one report).
  2. Click ⤓ Export Audit or ⤓ Export Report at the top right.
  3. Open the downloaded file in Word.

To place the export on CREDICORP letterhead:

  1. Open your master report template and use Save As to create a new file.
  2. Delete the old body below the cover page and Table of Contents. Keep the cover, the Table of Contents and the headers and footers — those carry the logo and the classification banner.
  3. In the exported file, press Ctrl+A then Ctrl+C, and paste beneath the Table of Contents in your template. Headings, colour-coded ratings and the findings table carry across.
  4. Click the Table of Contents, press F9 and choose Update entire table.

Section 3

Observations

An observation is a single audit finding: what was found, the criteria it breaches, its impact, its root cause, and the recommendation. Every observation must have an action owner accountable for remediating it.

3.1Raise an observation

There are three ways to start. All three lead into the same two-step wizard covered in 3.2 and 3.3.

From the dashboard

  1. On the Dashboard, click + New Observation.
  2. Type your one-liner observation — a plain sentence describing what you found. For example: Loan disbursements approved without evidence of credit committee sign-off.
  3. Select the Department and enter the Process / audit area.
  4. Select the Target report the observation belongs to.
  5. Click Generate observation. AI expands your one-liner into a full draft observation.
Note. If no report exists yet, the dialog says so and links to Audits & Reports. Create an audit and a report first — see 2.1 and 2.4.

From a report

  1. Open the report.
  2. Click + Add observation.

From a failed test

  1. Open the audit engagement.
  2. In the test programme, find a test whose Result is Exception or Partial.
  3. Click the raise-exception action on that test. The test's findings are carried into the draft.

3.2Review and edit the draft

Step 1 of the wizard is Review & edit observation. Check everything before assigning an owner.

FieldWhat it holds
RefRead-only. Assigned automatically and unique across all reports.
Criticality *Critical, High, Moderate, Low or Process Improvement. See Appendix A.
Category / themeThe control theme, used to group findings.
Title *The finding, stated in one line.
Detailed descriptionThe condition — what was found.
Criteria / expectationThe policy, standard or control the condition is measured against.
Impact / riskThe exposure the finding creates.
Possible root causeWhy it happened — process, people, system or governance.
RecommendationWhat Internal Audit recommends.

Click Next: assign owner → to continue.

Note. The Ref field cannot be edited. References are issued automatically and are unique across every report, so the same reference can never identify two different findings.

3.3Assign owners and timing

Step 2 is Raise observation — assign owner.

  1. Select the Primary action owner *. This is the department head who will respond to the observation and mark it Ready for Closure.
  2. Optionally select a Secondary action owner. Co-owners are notified, can follow the remediation, can comment, and can mark the observation Ready for Closure just as the primary owner can.
  3. Select the Resolution timeline — Immediate, Short-term or Long-term.
  4. Set the Closure date. It must fall after the report date.
  5. If this repeats a prior finding, see 3.4.
  6. Submit:
    • As Head of Audit, click Raise & notify owner. The observation is raised immediately and the action owner is notified.
    • As Audit Staff, click Submit for approval. The observation goes to the Head of Audit and the owner is not notified until it is approved.
Important. An observation cannot be raised without a primary action owner — there would be nobody accountable for remediating it, and it could never be closed. If the owner list is empty, no departments with heads have been set up yet; see 9.2.

3.4Repeat findings

  1. In step 2 of the wizard, tick Repeat observation (raised in a prior audit).
  2. In Repeat of, search prior observations and select the finding this one repeats.
  3. Alternatively, click AI scan for repeats to compare this observation against prior findings in other reports and flag a likely match. Confirm the result yourself before submitting.
Note. An observation marked as a repeat must name the prior finding it repeats. Repeat findings are counted on the dashboard and shown in reports with a ↻ REPEAT marker.

3.5Edit, reassign or delete

Open an observation from a report, the tracker or a notification. The controls at the top right depend on your role.

ActionHead of AuditAudit Staff
Edit the observationEdit — applied immediatelyPropose edit — sent to the Head for approval
Delete the observationDelete — applied immediatelyRequest deletion — sent to the Head for approval
Reassign the action ownerReassign ownerNot available

While a staff request is waiting, the observation shows ⏳ Change pending approval. Deletions are recorded in the audit trail.

3.6Proposed SOP updates

An observation can carry the change to the standard operating procedure that would prevent it recurring.

  1. Open the observation.
  2. Open Proposed SOP update.
  3. Type the proposed update, or upload the current SOP as a PDF and click ✦ Generate proposed SOP updates to draft it.
  4. Review, edit and save.

Each report shows a roll-up of how many of its observations have an SOP update drafted and how many are still outstanding.

Section 4

Remediation & Closure

Every observation is closed through the same four-stage workflow. Each stage is performed by a different party, and the stage you are at determines which buttons you are offered. The Remediation & verification block on the observation shows where the item stands and what happens next.

4.1The closure workflow

Stage 1
Raised
Internal Audit raises the observation and assigns the action owner.
Stage 2
Ready for closure
The action owner remediates, responds and attaches evidence.
Stage 3
Auditor verified
Internal Audit reviews the response and verifies it for closure.
Stage 4
Head verified & closed
The Head of Audit signs off and the observation is closed.

The stepper on each observation marks completed stages with ✓ and names who completed them and when. A stage that has been sent back is marked ↩ returned rather than reverting to blank, so a rejection is never mistaken for a restart.

Beneath the stepper, a Next step line always states what has to happen next and who has to do it. If you are unsure of your position on an item, read that line first.

Figure 5 — insert screenshot
Figure 5: The Remediation & verification block, showing the four-stage stepper, the conversation and the stage actions.

4.2Mark an observation Ready for Closure Action Owner

  1. Click Internal Observations in the Portal group of the side navigation bar, or open the item from your dashboard or a notification.
  2. Open the observation and read the description, criteria, impact and recommendation.
  3. Carry out the remediation in your department.
  4. Click ✓ Ready for Closure.
  5. Describe what your department did to address the finding.
  6. Attach supporting evidence.
  7. Submit. The observation moves to stage 2 and Internal Audit is notified to verify it.
Note. Both the primary and any secondary action owner can respond and mark an observation Ready for Closure. To add a comment without closing anything, use the comment box on the observation instead.

4.3Verify the remediation Internal Audit

Available to the Head of Audit, the engagement's lead auditor, and the auditor who raised the item.

  1. Open the observation. The action owner's response is shown in the conversation.
  2. Click View remediation.
  3. Review the owner's response and their evidence.
  4. Decide:
    • Verify & send for closure — add your closure note and send the item to the Head of Audit for sign-off.
    • Send back to owner — reject the response with a note explaining what more is needed. The observation returns to the action owner.

4.4Verify & close Head of Audit

  1. Open the observation once the auditor has verified it.
  2. Choose one of three outcomes:
    • Verify & close — add your comment and close the observation. It is stamped with the closure date and moves to Closed.
    • Reject to auditor — return it to Internal Audit with a note. The auditor addresses the note and resends it for closure verification.
    • Escalate to owner — send it back to the action owner for further work.
Note. Where the Head of Audit is also the lead auditor or raised the observation, an item rejected to the auditor comes back to them, and they are offered Resend for closure verification.

4.5Chasing a response Internal Audit

While an observation is waiting on the action owner, two requests are available on the observation:

  1. Request feedback from owner — notifies the owner that a response is expected. The button then reads 🔔 Update requested.
  2. Request progress report — asks for an interim update without closing anything. A banner stays on the observation until the owner marks it Ready for Closure.

To chase several owners at once, use 🔔 Remind owners on the Remediation Tracker (see Section 5).

4.6Disputing an observation Action Owner

Where an action owner believes an observation is not valid, it can be put forward for withdrawal. Withdrawal requires the Head of Audit's sign-off.

  1. Open the observation and click Request review.
  2. State the reason the observation is considered invalid, and submit.
  3. Internal Audit reviews the request and either:
    • Forwards it for withdrawal — it goes to the Head of Audit for sign-off; or
    • Declines the request — the observation remains active and the reason is recorded.
  4. Where it is forwarded, the Head of Audit either approves the withdrawal or does not. Both outcomes are recorded on the observation with the reason given.
Note. A withdrawn observation is neither Open nor Closed. It is retained in full with its reason and sign-off, and is listed separately from the active observations on the report.

Remediation Tracker

The tracker is the single view of every open action across the whole portfolio, and the analysis of what those actions have in common.

5.1Open actions

  1. Click Remediation Tracker in the side navigation bar.
  2. The Open Actions table lists every observation that is not yet closed, with its action or observation title, source audit and report, criticality, owner, process or department, age, expected close date, whether it is a repeat, and its status.
  3. Filter by criticality, status, owner or area, and click any row to open the observation.

5.2Audit insights

  1. On the Remediation Tracker, switch to Audit Insights.
  2. Review:
    • Risk Heat Map — process or department against criticality, showing where risk concentrates.
    • Recurring Root-Cause Themes — the themes running through findings, with counts.
    • Recent observations — the latest activity across the portfolio.

5.3Remind action owners Head of Audit

  1. On the Remediation Tracker, click 🔔 Remind owners.
  2. Select the owners to remind.
  3. Send. Each owner receives a notification and an email listing the items outstanding against them.

5.4Exports

  1. Click ⤓ Export tracker to export the open-actions view, or ⤓ Export insights while viewing Audit Insights.
  2. Click ⤓ All observations (Excel) to export every observation in the system to a spreadsheet.

Section 6

Risk & Assessment Modules

Five registers sit alongside the core audit loop. The Head of Audit sees all five; audit staff see only those granted to them individually in Settings.

6.1Audit Risk Assessment

Holds the audit universe — every auditable unit, risk-rated — and the annual risk-based audit plan built from it.

Build the audit universe

  1. Click Audit Risk Assessment in the side navigation bar.
  2. To draft the universe, click Generate audit universe, describe the Organisation context / areas, and generate. Review everything produced.
  3. To add a unit yourself, click + Add auditable unit and complete Auditable unit *, Category, Owner, the risk-factor scores, Last audited (year), and the optional Rating override and Frequency override.
  4. Add the Rationale / notes that justify the rating for the plan.
  5. Save.

Units are rated Low, Medium, High or Extreme. Use the edit (✎) and delete icons on a row to amend or remove a unit.

Create the annual audit plan

  1. Click + New audit plan.
  2. Enter the Plan year * and create it.
  3. To draft the plan from the universe, click Generate annual audit plan and add any Constraints / context. Review the result.
  4. Schedule units into periods and record the planned timing against each.
  5. Click ⤓ at the top right to download the plan.
Note. Creating, editing and deleting units and plans is restricted to the Head of Audit. Audit staff with access to this section can view it and download the plan.

6.2Fraud Risk Assessment

The fraud risk register, scored on likelihood and impact, with the preventive actions raised against each risk.

  1. Click Fraud Risk in the side navigation bar.
  2. Click + Add fraud risk. Step 1 captures the risk:
    • Year, Scheme category (ACFE) and Department
    • Fraud scheme / risk title * and Description (how the fraud could occur)
    • Likelihood (1–5), Impact (1–5) and Control strength
    • Existing anti-fraud controls
  3. Continue to step 2 and add the Prevention / response actions, each with a Control type, Owner, Target date and Status.
  4. Save.

To draft a register for a department, click Generate fraud risks, choose the Department, add any Context, and review what is produced. Use ⤓ Download for the register, and the Fraud Prevention Plan and Quarterly update views for reporting.

Note. Prevention actions assigned to a department head appear in that person's Fraud Risk Control Tracker in the Portal (see Section 8).

6.3Process Review

Reviews a documented SOP against the process as it actually runs, and records the findings and the proposed replacement process.

  1. Click Process Review in the side navigation bar.
  2. Click + New process review.
  3. Complete Department / business unit *, SOP title and Period / version.
  4. Upload the SOP document (PDF) * and create the review.
  5. Record the process steps, each with its Actor / role and Type.
  6. Add findings with + Add finding — Title *, Category, Severity, Detail and Recommendation.
  7. Record the Overall effectiveness rating, Summary and Key recommendations in Edit review details.

Click Generate proposed updated process to draft the revised process from the findings. To escalate a process finding into a full audit observation, open the finding, click Raise finding as observation and choose the Target report — the observation wizard in 3.2 takes over.

6.4External Findings Register

Holds findings raised by external bodies — regulators, external auditors, consultants — and tracks them to closure through the same owner workflow as internal observations.

  1. Click External Findings in the side navigation bar.
  2. Click + Log Finding.
  3. Complete the details: Source (external body), Source report / ref, Year, Severity, Finding / observation *, Detail, Risk / impact and Recommendation.
  4. Assign the Owner and set the Target date and Status.
  5. Where the finding recurs, tick Repeat finding? and record Repeat of (prior audit / year).
  6. On closure, record Verified by, Evidence / ref and the Closed date.

Use Import CSV to load a register in bulk, Export CSV to extract it, and AI Summary to draft commentary across the register. Switch between the Register and Insights views from the toolbar.

6.5IA Self-Assessment

Assesses the internal audit function against the IIA Global Internal Audit Standards (2024).

  1. Click IA Self-Assessment in the side navigation bar.
  2. Click + New assessment.
  3. Work through each standard. For each, record Conformance, Evidence of conformance, Gap / non-conformance, and any Improvement action with its Action owner, Target date, Status and Progress update.
  4. Rate each principle on Maturity (1–5) and add Principle-level commentary and the Key improvement action.
  5. Click Generate overall conclusion to draft the EQA-style opinion from the ratings and maturity recorded, then review and edit it.
  6. Click ✓ Mark complete when the assessment is finished. Use Reopen to amend a completed one.
  7. Click ⤓ Word to export.

The panel shows the number of standards rated and the average maturity score. Improvement actions are tracked against their owners and target dates.

Section 7

Oversight

Two sections available to the Head of Audit: the approvals queue that governs what audit staff may change, and the Executive Assurance Brief issued to the MD and EXCO.

7.1Approvals Head of Audit

Everything audit staff submit arrives here. The sidebar count shows how many requests are waiting.

  1. Click Approvals in the side navigation bar.
  2. The Pending approvals table lists each request with its type, the item, who requested it and when.
  3. Click a request to open it and review the detail. For an edit request, the proposed change is shown against the current value field by field.
  4. Approve or reject the request. A rejection carries a note back to the requester.
Request typeRaised whenEffect of approval
New observationAudit staff raise an observationThe observation becomes active and the action owner is notified.
Edit requestAudit staff propose an editThe proposed values replace the current ones.
Delete requestAudit staff request a deletionThe observation is deleted and the deletion recorded in the audit trail.
Status changeA status change needs sign-offThe new status is applied.
Withdrawal requestInternal Audit forwards an owner's review requestThe observation is withdrawn.
Plan completionAn engagement is marked complete on the annual planThe engagement is recorded as completed.

Decisions are listed under Recent decisions with the outcome, who decided and when. Use the All requests, Pending, Approved and Rejected filters to review history.

7.2Executive Assurance Brief Head of Audit

A periodic assurance summary for the MD/CEO and EXCO, drawn from the current state of the portfolio.

  1. Click Executive Assurance Brief in the side navigation bar.
  2. Click Key message to set the message the brief should lead with. This is optional.
  3. Click + Generate brief.
  4. Review the generated brief and edit it before it is issued.
  5. Share the brief using its link, or delete it to withdraw that link.

Briefs are listed by Year and Month. Recipients are maintained in Settings under MD & EXCO brief recipients (see 9.3), and are sent the brief automatically on the 1st and 15th of each month.

Important. A brief has a public link — anyone holding it can open the brief without signing in. Share it only with its intended recipients. Deleting the brief stops the link working.

Section 8

Action Owner Portal

This section is for department heads holding an Action Owner account. The Portal group in your side navigation bar holds everything assigned to you. A red count on an item shows how many are still open and awaiting your response.

8.1Internal Observations

  1. Click Internal Observations in the Portal group.

Administration

Settings and the Audit log are reached from the ⋮ menu beside your name at the bottom of the side navigation bar. Both are restricted to the Head of Audit.

9.1User access management

Add a user

  1. Open Settings from the ⋮ menu.
  2. In User access management, click + Add user.
  3. Start typing a staff name to pick the person from the corporate directory, or enter them manually.
  4. Complete Name *, Department, Email * and Role.
  5. For an Audit Staff account, tick the assessment sections that user should see in their side navigation bar.
  6. Save. The user signs in with their Microsoft account.

Edit a user, or change what they can see

  1. In User access management, click the user's row, or the edit icon (✎) on that row.
  2. Amend their details or their sidebar access.
  3. Save.

Make a user inactive, or active again

  1. On the user's row, click the power icon (⏻) to make the account inactive.
  2. Confirm. Sign-in is blocked and any open session ends immediately. The account, their observations and their full history are kept.
  3. To restore access, click the restore icon (⟳) on the row and confirm.
Note. Making a user inactive is the correct way to remove access. It preserves the audit trail. Permanent deletion is available but should be used only where an account was created in error.

9.2Departments & action owners

Each department's head becomes an Action Owner login and can be assigned observations.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. In Departments & action owners, click + Add Action Owner.
  3. Enter the Department name *.
  4. Enter the head's Name * — start typing to select them from the corporate directory — and their Email *.
  5. Save. The account is created and a welcome email sent.

To amend a department, click its row and choose Edit department. To remove one, use the delete action and confirm.

Important. Removing a department also makes its head's Action Owner login inactive, unless another department still points at the same person. Their history is kept, and the account can be made active again from User access management.

9.3MD & EXCO brief recipients

  1. Open Settings.
  2. In MD & EXCO brief recipients, click + Add MD & EXCO recipient.
  3. Enter the recipient's name and email, and save.

Recipients receive the Executive Assurance Brief automatically on the 1st and 15th of each month. Use Edit recipient to amend an entry, or remove one to stop the brief being sent to them.

9.4Audit log

A complete, non-editable record of who did what in AuditLens.

  1. Open Audit log from the ⋮ menu.
  2. Search by user or summary text, and filter by action.
  3. Click Search, or press Enter in the search box.
  4. Click ⤓ Export (Excel) to export the log, or Refresh to reload it.

Recorded actions include sign-in and sign-out; user accounts created, updated, made inactive, made active and deleted; audits, reports and observations created, updated and deleted; approval decisions; observations raised, verified and closed; action owners removed; and data exported or imported.

Note. The audit log cannot be edited or cleared from within AuditLens. Exporting it is itself recorded as an entry.

Appendix A

Risk Classification Guide

Every observation carries one of these five ratings. Apply them consistently — the rating drives the dashboard, the report's findings summary and the assurance opinion.

RatingDefinition
CriticalImmediate remediation Contravenes the Corporation's zero risk-tolerance indicators and risk appetite statement. Requires immediate remediation. Any auditable unit found in breach is automatically assigned an adverse opinion, as is a unit carrying numerous high-risk observations leading to a negative composite score.
HighImmediate remediation Significant vulnerability with potentially severe consequences if unaddressed — a major control failure, a regulatory breach or a critical weakness. Requires immediate action and close monitoring by executive management and the Board Audit Committee.
ModerateShort-term, 1–3 months Notable weakness causing inefficiencies and moderate financial or reputational harm if unresolved. Timely corrective action prevents escalation and protects operational integrity and compliance.
LowMedium-term, 4–6 months Minor control weakness or process deviation with minimal operational impact. Does not significantly increase risk exposure and is manageable through routine improvement.
Process
ImprovementFlexible timing		 An opportunity to improve efficiency and adopt best practice rather than a risk. Recommendations focus on optimising workflows and resource utilisation.
